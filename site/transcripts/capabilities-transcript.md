# Scheme: the work stays on your computer

A capabilities film using real Scheme on clean Ubuntu demonstration desktops and fictional example projects. Narration was generated locally. Phone footage is browser touch emulation, not a physical-device test. Cloud choices are shown without signing in or generating a paid response. Tailscale is a documented route, not a completed account demonstration.

## 00:00 — Leave the screen. Keep the work.

Close the browser. Come back later. The work is still here. Scheme puts the terminals on your computer within reach of the screen in front of you.

## 00:14 — One computer. Your whole workspace.

Your projects live on the host. Your tools run there too. Another desktop, a laptop, or a smaller screen becomes a window into that workspace through your private connection. The viewing device only needs a browser.

## 00:31 — Give every task its own place.

Start with a plain Shell. Choose a project folder, name the session, and get to work. Keep your launch checks in one tab and your next idea in another. Here, a real example project runs its tests. Switch tasks without losing your place.

## 00:52 — Find the thread again.

When the tabs multiply, search the session overview. Find a familiar name or project. Supported tools can also supply request excerpts and observed actions. The useful details stay beside the session, so getting back to work takes less hunting.

## 01:09 — Bring the tools you choose.

Use Shell on its own, or choose Claude Code, Codex, or a local model when installed. Your own accounts, sign-ins, and hardware power those tools. Scheme brings the terminals together. The local option checks its endpoint and model first, and does not silently fall back to the cloud.

## 01:33 — See what the tools report.

Keep supported activity, project state, model information, and usage close to the terminal. These are observations from your tools, and some may be missing or delayed. Unknown stays unknown. It does not mean idle, zero usage, or spare quota.

## 01:51 — Put a visual reference beside the work.

Have a screenshot to work from? Attach it to the current project. Scheme stores the upload on your host, where your terminal tools can use it. Review the file and its destination before uploading.

## 02:07 — Make room to concentrate.

Choose light, dark, or the device's own preference. Keep the side panel open for context, or hide it for more terminal space. The terminal stays dark so its colors remain readable.

## 02:20 — Reach it through your private connection.

The Connect view explains how to reach your host from another device. This demonstration uses an actual private S S H tunnel between two desktop machines. A private Tailscale route is also documented for remote access. Scheme runs on your computer; the public website is the guide.

## 02:43 — Keep the host working.

Closing the browser detaches the screen. The host can keep working. In this Linux demonstration, restarting Scheme's web service also leaves the same terminal running, and its job finishes. Keep the host awake. A reboot ends running processes, even when saved tabs or supported conversations can reopen.

## 03:09 — Read comfortably on a smaller screen.

On a smaller touch screen, Read turns recent terminal output into a readable snapshot. Enlarge the text, change wrapping, copy it, or request more history. Refresh when you want a new snapshot. This is the real responsive interface shown in browser touch emulation.

## 03:31 — Think first. Send when you are ready.

Write gives each session its own draft. Compose the thought before it reaches the terminal. Insert only lets you inspect the text first. Send and Enter submits it. Here, both actions are demonstrated with a harmless Shell command. Drafts are temporary browser storage, so save important work properly.

## 03:56 — Keep the useful controls within reach.

The touch key bar keeps terminal keys nearby. Open Tools for text size, theme, and Focus. The same workspace adapts to the available screen.

## 04:08 — Your computer. Your tools. Any screen.

One place for the work. More freedom in how you reach it. Scheme is free, open source, and self-hosted. Explore the capabilities, watch the setup walkthrough, and start with your first Shell session.
